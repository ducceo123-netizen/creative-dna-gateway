# UID LDP: brief-led UI adaptation

Use this reference when the user requests a UID Brands landing-page design. Transfer the ability to follow the brief and shopper need, not the visual skin or fixed section sequence of a previous demo. This is host design guidance; it does not change canonical records, route schemas, permissions, or existing outcomes.

## Resolve the actual workflow

Enter this UID LDP mode before any general Extract/brand-library mode. Do not require a full three-brand extraction, component-library rebuild or new brand identity to execute an LDP brief. The existing website-brand-ui `scripts/render.cjs` produces brand-manual documents and REVIEW.html from brand.json; it is not an LDP renderer and its successful execution is not design-draft or visual-QA evidence.

Read the current resolver output for the exact brand and LDP branch before planning. Use its current delivery and validation contract, not a remembered Figma or hosted-HTML workflow. A listed canonical route is not proof that a full LDP planner/renderer supports that brand. Never submit another brand under PawfectHouse or borrow its baseline to pass a validator.

At the 2026-10-30 inspection, PawfectHouse `ldp-system` has a full design-draft workflow; GiftSoul has a listed canonical LDP route but no equivalent workflow contract in the inspected response; SoulPrise has no listed LDP-system route. These are observations to recheck, not permanent routing constants. Where a workflow is unsupported, identify the gap and keep independent source research useful; do not manufacture a canonical route.

Current accepted scoped rules and user locks take priority over stylistic recommendations here. Resolve selected material rules separately, including hero CTA placement, mobile composition, dimensions, baked text and image restrictions. Do not import Home-hero CTA behavior into an LDP hero. Do not impose the five-section Onepage format on a collection landing page.

## Convert the brief into design decisions

For a collection-specific brief, follow this evidence-to-outcome pipeline before assembling the UI:

1. **Decompose the real collection.** Inspect its pagination and assortment; group verified products by product type, recipient, occasion, interest and meaningful personalization choices. Record coverage, overlaps, exceptions and missing facts. Do not infer an entire collection from one featured SKU.
2. **Learn how existing same-brand LDPs decompose a niche.** Inspect actual pages and images to identify taxonomy, grouping labels, shopper journey, section roles, ordering, merchandising density and mobile treatment. Separate reusable decision logic from campaign-specific products, claims and visual treatment.
3. **Research the market and competitor composition.** Inspect relevant real landing pages for the same buyer/occasion/product context. Record direct sources, observation time, accessed sections and limits; compare how they partition content, introduce products, sequence navigation and story, and handle layouts. Search-result snippets alone do not establish visual inspection. Use permitted market/community evidence for bounded shopper hypotheses, never proven demand or conversion.
4. **Compare their pattern with our actual assortment.** Build a small matrix of shopper need, observed external pattern, our eligible products/evidence, missing coverage and possible original adaptation. A competitor's claim, category, price, review or delivery promise does not transfer to our store.
5. **Decide reuse / adapt / reject.** Reuse a same-brand module when its role and content fit. Adapt its grouping, composition or imagery when supported by our assortment and brand DNA. Reject patterns that require nonexistent products, unsupported claims, inaccessible interactions or conflict with current canonical/user locks. Record one concrete reason per material decision, rather than copying competitor section order automatically.
6. **Apply the UI skill.** Convert those decisions into the brief-to-section map, brand-specific layout rhythm, mobile behavior, content hierarchy and asset plan described below. Do not start with an attractive template and retrofit unrelated products into it.
7. **Visualize the actual outcome.** Produce and inspect complete desktop/mobile drafts with final copy and real mapped assets, then deliver the current workflow's exact mapping. A research summary, a plan or a successful brand-manual script is not the requested visual outcome.

If market/competitor access is blocked or sampling is partial, preserve the limit and continue independent source-backed work. Do not fabricate a completed research stage or bypass access restrictions. Ask only if an essential unresolved gap prevents a defensible section or final outcome.

Keep a small task-local decision map, outside strict gateway JSON unless its schema explicitly accepts it:

`brief requirement → shopper need → selected section/element → content and image role → source or declared hypothesis → viewport behavior → acceptance check`.

Separate explicit user requirements, verified product facts, observed brand grammar, and design hypotheses. “Demand” means the user's requested purpose or a bounded shopper hypothesis; it does not imply measured market demand, conversion or a winning product. Reuse current verified research; do not recrawl unchanged evidence without a concrete gap or freshness need.

Choose the page around the actual assortment and journey. A discovery brief may need clear niche/product navigation and comparable product cards early. A narrow gift-selection brief may need a concise recipient/occasion explanation before browsing. A single-product story is not an acceptable substitute for a requested multi-product LDP. Do not invent a fixed card count, extra categories, urgency, reviews or policy reassurance to make the page look complete.

Every section must earn its place: what decision does it help the shopper make, what verified items can it display, and where does its action lead? Remove decorative repetitions that do not advance that journey. Keep a deliberate rhythm of merchandise, editorial imagery and navigation rather than repeating the same grid or banner. Use full page hierarchy and readable copy, not a collection of isolated pretty modules.

## Preserve brand expression without copying defects

Read same-brand live examples at component and assembly level: title/body/price roles, contextual surfaces, icon grammar, image ratios, spacing, density, section pairing and transitions. A repeated functional role does not prove identical section order or visual treatment across brands. A same-brand module may have a different campaign skin. Keep exact theme variable names when verified; keep price, primary action and selection colors semantically distinct even when their values coincide.

Use the actual brand font or disclose an unavailable font; never silently substitute and call the result faithful. Do not carry COURTSIDE's condensed sport display type, PAWPOP's purple/lime, or a new-brand mockup palette into an established UID brand. Keep neutral buying/browsing surfaces and reserve stronger colors for roles supported by brand evidence. Do not recolor every multicolor illustration into one accent.

Learn layout anatomy separately from product claims. Source Onepage text can conflict with the actual SKU form or gallery. Preserve only eligible facts and exact personalization fields. Generated lifestyle images are commercial styling mockups, not customer reviews, manufacturing evidence or product specification proof.

## Mobile-first composition

Compose the smallest supported intended viewport first, then extend to desktop. Use 390px as a working view only when consistent with the active baseline; inspect 320/390/430 layouts where the render tool supports them. Report which widths were actually inspected. Do not change a version-bound baseline width merely to fit a preferred screenshot.

Keep essential choices visible, body copy readable and controls comfortably operable. Use a proposed 44×44px minimum hit area for actionable targets, with adequate separation; small visible icons may sit inside larger transparent targets. This is a design recommendation, not a claim that the source store already complies. Avoid long labels wrapping into tiny buttons, page-wide horizontal overflow, accidental crop of faces/product artwork, and a sticky action area covering the last item.

Choose the renderer by purpose:

- Grid: compare a small set of categories or product options; use a visible 2×2 arrangement for four essential choices when it remains readable.
- Carousel: optional exploration or larger editorial/product groups where swipe/scroll is actually implemented. Show a next-card peek, legible card width and usable controls; no automatic rotation is needed. Essential choices must not become unreachable in a static draft.
- Editorial split/stack: explain one idea with one supporting image; stack in a sensible reading order on mobile instead of shrinking the desktop composition.

If the current gateway layout schema supports only stack/split/grid, do not insert an unsupported carousel enum or claim a static clipped row works. Choose a supported visible layout, or record a separately authorized renderer extension. A screenshot can demonstrate visual layout, not swipe, selection, upload or checkout behavior.

## Image and content planning

Assign each image a job: broad niche introduction, product comparison, exact technical detail, or a believable recipient moment. Keep product silhouette, construction, artwork and personalization consistent with viewed source references. For generated thumbnails, vary useful staging while maintaining a coherent art direction; avoid putting every product on the same pedestal. Use actual source technical images when generation would compromise factual detail.

Recompose desktop/mobile hero assets where the material contract requires a pair; do not rely on a destructive crop. Keep native editable text outside imagery as required. Verify text contrast against the actual local image region, not only the brand color swatch. Attach or map originals separately from compressed Figma/render previews and disclose their roles.

## Bind the design to the existing UID envelope

Populate existing `research.ldp_job.design_strategy` with the real idea, shopper journey, hierarchy, layout variation, mobile decisions, brand consistency and rationale. Put section-specific choices in `layout_intent` and `mobile_intent`. Maintain exact product, evidence, destination and asset IDs. Supply only fields accepted by the current schema; the decision map is a companion artifact, not a reason to weaken strict validation.

Use the current `ldp_preview_layout` schema and `ldp_asset_outputs` inventory. Bind actual task/content/canonical snapshots and freshly prepared mapping hash. Do not fake receipts, timestamps, image hashes or visual checks to satisfy structure. Render the full desktop and mobile design, inspect the exact output bytes, and only then provide truthful `ldp_design_draft` receipts when requested by the runtime contract.

The inspected current default is full desktop/mobile draft images plus exact mapping; local HTML may assist rendering. Do not add a hosted URL, deployment or Figma-creation gate unless the current user request and runtime require it. If Figma is explicitly requested, keep all work for that task in one tab, preserve unrelated pages, use native reusable components and readable agent descriptions, and follow required Figma tools/skills.

## Acceptance and revision

Verify the brief-to-output decision map against the actual full-page renders. Check: requested scope and assortment; brand fidelity; section purpose and order; source-eligible copy; complete asset inventory; mobile reading/order/targets; hero contrast and crops; full footer and below-fold content; and actual interaction coverage. Check long product names and multi-item browsing, not only short happy-path copy. Distinguish passed, untested, blocked and proposed improvements.

For feedback, resolve the exact task, content version, mapping hash, section/element IDs and viewport before editing. Preserve approved unrelated content and product truth. Re-render affected views and refresh mapping after changes. User approval of one sample is not approval of all brand rules, all assets, future output QC or canonical training. Report concrete improvement and remaining gaps without an invented EO score.
